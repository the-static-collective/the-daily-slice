# YĀḪÛ-ŠŪʿ — An External Vowel Witness at the Name Door

**Date:** 2026-09-17  
**Status:** `DOCUMENTED WITNESS` cuneiform onomastics · `INFERENCE` pronunciation pressure · `QUESTION` Jesus-name provenance

## Why this earned a slice

The current National Treasure teaser leaves one door explicitly unopened: **THE NAME OF JESUS**. The live conversation then asked a narrower provenance question before theology: what is the oldest externally vocalized evidence near the Hebrew name-family conventionally represented as Joshua / Yehoshua / Yeshua?

A strong extra-biblical witness is now on the table.

Kathleen Abraham's 2024 Cambridge chapter on Hebrew personal names in first-millennium BCE Babylonian cuneiform gives the spelling:

```text
ᵐ.d ia-ḫu-ú-šu-ú
```

normalized there as:

```text
Yāḫû-šūʿ
```

The example occurs in a table illustrating how Babylonian cuneiform renders Hebrew / West Semitic gutturals. The same chapter later includes **Yāḫû-šūʿ** in a Judean family tree from Yāhūdu.

This matters because cuneiform is syllabic enough to preserve vowel information that bare consonantal Hebrew `יהושע` does not directly supply.

Source: Kathleen Abraham, “Hebrew Names,” in *Personal Names in Cuneiform Texts from Babylonia (c. 750–100 BCE): An Introduction* (Cambridge University Press, 2024), pp. 139–165, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009291071.011

Direct Cambridge chapter: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/personal-names-in-cuneiform-texts-from-babylonia-c-750100-bce/hebrew-names/D311D2597B53E929381C5AB380DCA2DE

## The exact receipt

The strongest factual chain is deliberately smaller than the theological question:

```text
Babylonian cuneiform witness
    ↓
ia-ḫu-ú-šu-ú
    ↓ scholarly normalization
Yāḫû-šūʿ
```

Abraham also gives other initial Yahwistic names such as:

```text
Yāḫû-natan
Yāḫû-ʿizr(ī)
Yāḫû-kīn
```

and explains that cuneiform spellings of the Yahwistic element vary. On the same Nippur tablet, for example, a cuneiform `Yāḫû-natan` is also written alphabetically as `yhwntn`. This is unusually useful provenance because the syllabic and consonantal writing systems touch the same naming tradition.

The chapter explicitly cautions that the original Hebrew articulation of the divine name remains uncertain and that cuneiform spellings are multiple and sometimes confusing. It therefore does **not** authorize one reconstructed pronunciation of YHWH from this evidence alone.

## What the witness establishes

**Documented:** A first-millennium BCE Hebrew/Jahwistic personal-name corpus from Babylonia contains a syllabically written `ia-ḫu-ú-šu-ú`, normalized by a modern specialist as `Yāḫû-šūʿ`. The name occurs among Judean-descended bearers in the Yāhūdu material.

**Documented:** The same corpus independently attests initial `Yāḫû-` in numerous Hebrew/Jahwistic compound names.

**Documented:** The Cambridge treatment warns against simply back-projecting later Tiberian/Masoretic vocalization into the cuneiform evidence; normalization can instead stay close to the cuneiform, use historical-linguistic reconstruction, or use later biblical parallels, and those are different operations.

## What it does NOT establish

```text
Yāḫû-šūʿ attested
!=
Jesus of Nazareth personally attested in Babylonian cuneiform
```

The tablet concerns another ancient bearer of the name-family centuries before Jesus.

```text
Yāḫû-šūʿ attested
!=
proof that every יהו- name in every Hebrew dialect and century began Yāhû-
```

The cuneiform corpus itself shows variation, and Hebrew changed across region and time.

```text
Yāḫû-šūʿ attested
!=
proof of the original pronunciation of YHWH
```

Abraham explicitly keeps that question open.

```text
Yāḫû-šūʿ attested
!=
proof that first-century Jesus was ordinarily called exactly “Yahushua”
```

That requires a separate transmission argument across centuries, dialects, Hebrew/Aramaic contraction, Greek `Ἰησοῦς`, and first-century naming practice.

## The provenance consequence

The witness nevertheless changes the search geometry.

Before this receipt, one might compare only:

```text
unpointed Hebrew consonants
יהושע

vs.

later Masoretic vocalization
Yəhôšuaʿ / Yehoshua
```

That leaves the early vowel question heavily underdetermined.

The Babylonian evidence adds a third road:

```text
Hebrew consonantal tradition
        ↘
         NAME FAMILY
        ↗
Babylonian syllabic hearing / writing
```

The second road does not replace the Hebrew witness. It constrains it differently.

Candidate research rule:

> **WHEN THE SOURCE SCRIPT SUPPRESSES A FEATURE, SEEK AN INDEPENDENT CONTEMPORARY WRITING SYSTEM THAT WAS FORCED TO REPRESENT IT.**

That is a provenance method, not a pronunciation verdict.

## ALEX / 3rdi pressure

This is almost a textbook observer-local truth specimen.

The Hebrew consonantal witness preserves one set of distinctions very well and vowels poorly:

```text
יהושע
```

The Babylonian syllabary preserves some vowel information but has its own lossy mappings, especially for West Semitic gutturals and consonants absent from Akkadian.

So neither surface is “the audio.”

They are two projections with different loss functions.

```text
SOURCE PRONUNCIATION
   ↓ Hebrew alphabetic projection
Y-H-W-Š-ʿ

SOURCE PRONUNCIATION
   ↓ Babylonian syllabic projection
ia-ḫu-ú-šu-ú
```

The useful move is not to crown one projection. It is to intersect their constraints while keeping each writing system's distortions explicit.

This yields a strong research seal:

> **THE SECOND WITNESS IS VALUABLE BECAUSE IT LOSES DIFFERENT THINGS.**

## The Jesus-name frontier

The next historical chain should be built as separate dated receipts rather than one backward reconstruction:

```text
pre-/exilic Hebrew יהושע family
    ↓
6th–5th c. BCE cuneiform Yāḫû-šūʿ witness
    ↓
post-exilic ישוע / Yeshua contraction evidence
    ↓
Second Temple Hebrew + Aramaic naming practice
    ↓
Greek Ἰησοῦς
    ↓
first-century Jesus of Nazareth
```

Each arrow is a research problem.

The most important unresolved discriminator is now:

> **Does the cuneiform Yāḫû-šūʿ belong historically and linguistically to the same יהושע / ישוע name lineage strongly enough to constrain the first-century form, and if so, how much?**

That should be answered from specialist onomastics and Second Temple evidence, not from devotional preference in either direction.

## Epistemic split

**Documented fact:** `ia-ḫu-ú-šu-ú = Yāḫû-šūʿ` is published in a 2024 Cambridge scholarly treatment of Hebrew names in Babylonian cuneiform; it is used as a Hebrew/Jahwistic example and appears in a Judean family tree from Yāhūdu.

**Inference:** This is materially stronger evidence for an ancient `Yāhû-`-like vocalization in this name-family than bare unpointed Hebrew consonants alone, because the Babylonian writing system records vowels syllabically.

**Counterevidence / limitation:** Cuneiform is itself an imperfect foreign-language projection; the corpus contains orthographic variation; the chapter explicitly states that the original articulation of the divine name is not known. The evidence therefore constrains but does not uniquely reconstruct pronunciation.

**Speculation:** This witness may eventually support a historical road closer to `Yāhû-šūʿ / Yahushua` than the later Masoretic `Yəhôšuaʿ`, but that conclusion is not yet earned for first-century Jesus. The intervening transmission chain must be built.

## Durable next questions

- Identify the exact tablet / publication behind `ia-ḫu-ú-šu-ú` and its date, provenance, bearer, and family context.
- Find all independent cuneiform attestations of the same name, not merely the same `Yāḫû-` prefix.
- Compare the Babylonian form against the Hebrew spellings `יהושע`, `יהושוע`, and `ישוע` by securely dated witnesses.
- Track when and where initial `YHW-` theophoric names surface as `Yāhû-`, `Yəhô-`, `Yô-`, or other forms.
- Establish the first-century Judean/Galilean distribution of `Yeshua` versus longer forms.
- Then—and only then—ask what historical form is best supported for Jesus of Nazareth.
