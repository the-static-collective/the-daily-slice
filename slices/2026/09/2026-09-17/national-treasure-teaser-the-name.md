## The relation-rewrite aperture

A useful abstraction emerged from sympathetic / representational magic.

Given a representation or trace `E` and a fact / target `F`:

```text
E --represents / names / came-from / belonged-to--> F
```

an operation introduces some intended relation `R` to an idea or state `I`:

```text
E --R--> I
```

The controversial magical claim can then be represented as a promotion rule:

```text
E --resolves-to--> F
E --R--> I
-----------------
F --R--> I    ?
```

This does not establish that such promotion is physically real. It isolates the exact disputed edge:

> **Which relations are allowed to propagate, under what conditions, and with what receipts?**

Known domains already answer this differently. Physics, biology, language, law, software, institutions, art, and culture all permit different kinds of relation propagation. Occult systems propose additional relations whose causal status remains open to test.

## Blood opened the next door

The blood-of-Jesus dig made the relation problem materially sharper.

Biblical and later Christian blood language carries overlapping relations to:

- life;
- covenant;
- purification;
- boundary;
- belonging;
- sacrifice;
- access;
- identity / embodied trace.

A particularly sharp historical specimen appears in the 1462 Franciscan–Dominican controversy over Christ's shed blood. The dispute asked whether blood physically separated from Christ during the Passion remained united to the divine person. The theological details differ by school, but structurally the question is almost exactly:

```text
A was bodily related to B
A becomes spatially separated from B
does R(A,B) persist?
```

That makes the medieval controversy a strong independent witness for a recurring ontology problem:

> **Physical separation does not automatically answer whether an identity-bearing relation has terminated.**

Source road: A. Fitzpatrick, “Mendicant order politics and the status of Christ's shed blood,” *Historical Research* 85 (2012), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2281.2011.00580.x

A second historical road treats medieval blood as deeply implicated in truth, integrity, identity, Eucharist, and access to Christ rather than as a simple symbol of death.

Source road: B. Bildhauer, “Medieval European conceptions of blood: truth and human integrity,” *Journal of the Royal Anthropological Institute* 19 (2013), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9655.12016

Candidate compression:

```text
blood = relation made material
```

Not canon. Not mechanism. A useful aperture.

## John through the aperture

Rereading Jesus' words in the Gospel of John through this relational lens exposed a striking recurring grammar:

```text
FACT
  -> SIGN
  -> WITNESS
  -> BELIEF
  -> ABIDING
  -> LIFE
  -> SENDING
```

John repeatedly works with verbs and relations such as:

```text
send
receive
hear
see
know
witness
believe
love
abide
be-in
be-one
```

The Gospel's authority language is strongly provenance-shaped:

```text
Father -> Son -> disciples -> world
```

Jesus repeatedly locates his words, works, judgment, and mission in relation to the Father who sent him. The disciples are later sent analogously.

John 6 then gives a particularly strong participation specimen:

```text
Father -> Son -> participant
```

with the reciprocal-abiding language of John 6:56–57 linking flesh/blood, life, and mutual indwelling.

John 14 and 17 expand the topology:

```text
Father in Son
Son in Father
human in Son
Son in human
believers becoming one
```

This suggests a candidate research phrase:

> **relation inheritance without identity collapse**

John 19:34–35 then gives a particularly Dogram-shaped sequence:

```text
event -> blood/water trace -> observer -> witness -> testimony -> belief
```

and John 20:30–31 states the Gospel's own transmission logic:

```text
signs -> selected witness -> writing -> reader -> belief -> life
```

The Gospel itself can therefore be studied as a **relation-preservation device across spatial and temporal separation**.

Relevant scholarly roads:

- A. D. Garton, “Before Abraham was, I am: A Theological Treatise on the Concept of Time in John's Gospel,” *Modern Theology* 35 (2019), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/moth.12476 — participatory divine-life / abiding reading.
- U. Heckel, “Where Now for Visible Unity?”, *The Ecumenical Review* 76 (2024), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/erev.12867 — mutual indwelling / reciprocal in-being in John 6, 15, and 17.
- J. Lookadoo, review of *Johannine Belief and Graeco-Roman Devotion*, *Reviews in Religion & Theology* 30 (2023), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/rirt.14220 — cognitive, relational, ethical, ongoing, and public dimensions of Johannine belief.

## The Green Grammar pressure

The conversation sharpened the existing candidate from:

```text
IDEA / FACT / RELATION
    -> encounter
    -> difference
    -> non-collapse
    -> creation
```

toward another possible formulation:

> **Creation is the lawful promotion of an idea into a new relation with fact.**

This is not adopted as a law.

Its usefulness is that it permits very different domains to be compared without declaring them equivalent:

```text
engineering  -> physically executable relation
law          -> institutionally authoritative relation
language     -> semantic / referential relation
software     -> executable symbolic relation
art          -> expressive relation
science      -> tested explanatory relation
magic        -> claimed occult causal relation
```

The discriminator becomes:

> **Which proposed relation survives the domain's actual admission tests?**

## The teaser turn: THE NAME

Once John was read this way, “the Name” stopped looking like a decorative religious phrase.

Johannine name-language clusters around:

- identity;
- representation;
- provenance;
- authority;
- belonging;
- protection;
- asking / acting;
- life;
- sending.

This opens the still-unresolved candidate:

```text
NAME != mere word-string
```

Possible decomposition:

```text
NAME
  ~= identity
   + provenance
   + authority
   + relation
   + representation
```

The live National Treasure question is therefore:

> **When Jesus says “in my name,” is the name functioning merely as an identifier, or as a relation that locates the speaker / actor inside a provenance chain: Father -> Son -> disciple -> world?**

If the latter survives textual pressure, “in my name” would look less like a verbal password and more like:

> **an operation whose claimed identity and authority remain relationally continuous with its source.**

That is the next locked door.

## Epistemic split

**Documented fact:** Historical Christian, Jewish, magical, sacramental, and devotional traditions contain robust concepts of names, blood, bodily traces, participation, covenant, witness, ritual relation, and persistence across separation. The Gospel of John repeatedly uses relational language of sending, receiving, knowing, abiding, mutual indwelling, witness, belief, life, and name. The 1462 blood controversy genuinely concerned whether Christ's shed blood remained united to the Deity after physical separation.  

**Inference:** These materials can be fruitfully compared as different answers to a common abstract question: how relations are established, preserved, transformed, inherited, or terminated. John's Gospel is especially amenable to representation as a provenance / participation graph.  

**Speculation:** `FACT / IDEA / RELATION` may describe a deeper “Green Grammar of Creation”; blood may be modeled as “relation made material”; “in Jesus' name” may encode a source-continuous relational / provenance position rather than functioning primarily as an uttered identifier. No occult causal mechanism, metaphysical law, or theological doctrine is established by these mappings.
