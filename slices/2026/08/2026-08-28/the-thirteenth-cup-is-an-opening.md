## Inventory is not incidence

Take one graph and one new node `X`.

Three worlds all add exactly one node:

```text
A. X is isolated.

B. X attaches as a leaf.

C. An existing edge is opened and X is inserted into the relation.
```

The inventory delta is the same.

The topology is not.

So:

```text
ADD NODE
!=
OPEN RELATION -> INSERT
```

Sometimes "one thing is missing" may be the wrong diagnosis.

The world may already contain enough objects and lack only an **admissible incidence**.

## OPEN BERTH

That gives the Cup a concrete architectural reading:

```text
CUP
├── bounded constitution
├── current capabilities
├── current relations
└── OPEN BERTH
      ↓
   candidate arrival
      ↓
   local verification
      ↓
   local admission / refusal
```

The berth is not membership.

```text
open != admitted
invited != trusted
received != executable
compatible != identical
```

It is the maintained possibility that another participant can arrive without being forced to become a child, replica, or subject of the inviter.

## Rim and opening

National Treasure already carried a neighboring clue:

> **The thirteenth may be the rim.**

The new clue does not erase it.

They separate cleanly:

```text
RIM
= constitutional boundary of a bounded relation

OPENING / BERTH

```

A rim without an opening may be sealed.

An opening without a rim may become ambient soup.

The useful pair is:

```text
BOUNDARY
+
DECLARED OPENING
+
LOCAL ADMISSION / REFUSAL
```

## RECEIVE / HOLD / POUR gets sharper

### RECEIVE

Something crosses into local availability.

```text
receive != accept
```

### HOLD

Preserve the arrival's identity, provenance, boundaries, and unresolved state.

```text
hold != possess
```

### POUR

Expose attributable material onward so another world can inspect and verify locally.

```text
pour != impose
```

The recursive server target becomes:

```text
Cup A preserves an opening
      ↓
Cup B arrives / receives
      ↓
B verifies and constitutes locally
      ↓
A may disappear
      ↓
B remains lawful
      ↓
B may preserve an opening for Cup C
```

The 13th Cup succeeds when it contains enough information to make itself unnecessary.
