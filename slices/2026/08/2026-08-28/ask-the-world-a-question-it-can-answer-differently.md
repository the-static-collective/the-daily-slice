# ASK THE WORLD A QUESTION IT CAN ANSWER DIFFERENTLY

**Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Status:** CANDIDATE

## What was noticed

The decoder work and merged `PROJECTION-BREAK-001` collided cleanly enough to expose two different epistemic acts that had been sharing one word.

A decoder may be passive:

```text
WORLD
  ↓
coordinate / observation rule
  ↓
REPRESENTATION
```

The world's tested trajectory does not change.

A probe is different:

```text
WORLD
+
chosen input
  ↓
CHANGED TRAJECTORY
  ↓
OBSERVATION
```

So:

> **A decoder tells you what a world looks like under a coordinate system. A probe asks the world a question it can answer differently.**

And the execution boundary is harder:

> **If your decoder can change the future, receipt it as part of the experiment.**

## Projection Break becomes an experimental method

`PROJECTION-BREAK-001` begins with two materially different worlds that are observer-equivalent at `T0`, then applies one shared attributable intervention and looks for the first lawful future divergence.

That suggests a general research loop:

```text
OBSERVE
  ↓
construct surviving candidate-world family
  ↓
find a bounded probe on which candidates predict different futures
  ↓
prove the challenge itself is sufficiently equivalent
  ↓
PRESSURE
  ↓
BREAK / INVARIANCE / FOG
  ↓
refine candidate family
  ↓
repeat
```

Observation tells us what worlds still look possible.

A discriminating intervention asks whether those worlds remain equivalent for a declared future question.

## The bounded conclusion

Suppose:

```text
D(Wa) = D(Wb)
```

at baseline.

Apply the same predeclared probe `u`:

```text
Wa --u--> Wa'
Wb --u--> Wb'
```

and observe:

```text
D(Wa') != D(Wb')
```

The safe conclusion is:

> **These worlds are not equivalent under this probe and observation constitution.**

Not:

> **The complete hidden causal mechanism has been discovered.**

Different futures break an equivalence before they prove a cause.

## The intervention itself has ancestry

A break is uninterpretable if the supposed shared question was not actually shared.

The paired challenge needs its own receipt:

```text
same intended input
same relevant parameters
same temporal placement
same admission conditions
same observer-visible stimulus
same measurement rule
same effect fence
```

A tiny hidden mismatch can manufacture the very divergence the test hopes to attribute to latent structure.

That gives the hostile companion `INTERVENTION-EQUIVALENCE-001`.

## One break is not enough

One probe may distinguish worlds without identifying why.

A stronger bounded signature is a small response vector:

```text
R(W) = <
  response_to_I1,
  response_to_I2,
  response_to_I3
>
```

The rival hypotheses should predict the pattern, not merely one lucky split.

An available probe that fails to separate the worlds also proves very little. Weak excitation is not identity.

```text
same response to u
!=
same hidden world
```

The question may simply have been useless.

```text
interesting

```
