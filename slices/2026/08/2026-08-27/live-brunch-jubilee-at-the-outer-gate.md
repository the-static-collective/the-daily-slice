## ALEX prediction test — lunar phase first

The Hebrew calendar fixes Yom Kippur at **10 Tishrei**, so it must occupy a repeatable phase of the lunar month.

A Wolfram ephemeris pass over **200 Yom Kippurs, Hebrew years 5661–5860 / Gregorian 1900–2099**, measured the interval from 10 Tishrei at 12:00 UTC to the next astronomical full moon.

Results:

```text
minimum:   3.03 days before full moon
maximum:   5.78 days before full moon
mean:      4.42 days before full moon
median:    4.38 days before full moon
std dev:   0.69 days
```

The same pass measured geocentric Sun–Moon ecliptic elongation:

```text
minimum:   107.48°
maximum:   145.33°
mean:      125.43°
median:    124.83°
```

This is the first hard survivor:

> **10 Tishrei is mechanically a repeatable approach-to-opposition phase, centered near about 4.4 days before full moon.**

That result is calendar mechanics, not hidden astronomy.

## The physical gate candidate — Earth's magnetotail boundary

Earth's magnetosphere is compressed on the Sun-facing side and drawn into a long antisolar magnetotail on the nightside.

NASA describes the magnetotail as the nightside extension of Earth's magnetosphere and emphasizes that the structure is dynamic and solar-wind dependent.

A classic NASA technical treatment of lunar transient phenomena gives useful nominal lunar-distance boundary timings:

```text
Moon encounters bow-shock front:
~4.5 days before full moon

Moon encounters magnetopause:
~2 days before full moon
```

Source:
https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19710021616/downloads/19710021616.pdf

Modern NASA visualization independently confirms the broader geometry: every orbit, the Moon passes into and out of Earth's magnetosphere around the full-moon portion of the lunation.

Source:
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5648/

The numerical coincidence that survived the first pressure pass is therefore specific:

```text
200-YEAR YOM KIPPUR MEAN
4.42 days before full moon

NOMINAL LUNAR BOW-SHOCK ENTRY
~4.5 days before full moon
```

This is close enough to earn an H1.

It does **not** mean the Moon crosses one fixed physical surface on every Yom Kippur. The magnetosphere and bow shock move with solar-wind conditions; lunar orbital latitude also matters; the calendar day spans many hours; and the 10-Tishrei phase itself ranges over nearly three days in the 200-year sample.

So:

```text
YOM KIPPUR = MAGNETOTAIL ENTRY
```

is **not admitted**.

The surviving statement is:

> **Yom Kippur repeatedly occupies the lunar phase neighborhood in which the Moon approaches the outer Earth-controlled solar-wind boundary on its way toward the magnetotail.**

## The second clock — seasonal phase

The test then asked whether a far-off planetary alignment recurs.

It does not.

The strong second invariant is simpler: Tishrei is also seasonally constrained. Across the same 200-year sample, the Sun's geocentric ecliptic longitude on 10 Tishrei remained approximately:

```text
171.84° to 201.43°
mean 186.40°
```

The September equinox is 180°.

So Yom Kippur/Jubilee sits at the intersection of two phase constraints:

```text
ANNUAL SOLAR CLOCK
near September equinox

        X

MONTHLY LUNAR CLOCK
~4.4 days before full / near nominal bow-shock approach
```

The outer planets do not supply a repeatable universal alignment. In a century-scale sample, Mars and the giant planets ranged through most or all 30° ecliptic sectors. Mercury and Venus remain seasonally near the Sun for ordinary orbital reasons, not because Jubilee forces a unique planetary configuration.

This is the boring explanation that strengthens rather than kills the useful survivor:

> **The gate is an intersection of recurring phase coordinates, not a once-a-year lineup of all planets.**
